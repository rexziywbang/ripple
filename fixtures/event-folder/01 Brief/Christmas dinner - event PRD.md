# Christmas dinner · Event PRD

Owner: People Operations  
Contributors: Workplace, Internal Communications, Finance, Production  
Version: 0.4 · September 18, 2026  
Planning status: Working brief

## Purpose

Bring the Northstar team together for an evening that feels generous, relaxed and personal. The dinner is the main event. Awards and the company update should punctuate the evening rather than turn it into a long all-hands meeting. Guests should leave having spent time with colleagues they do not normally work with.

The organizing team is small. The plan should avoid unnecessary custom production, complicated guest instructions and repeated vendor messages. A change to one arrangement should be reflected in the documents the affected people already use.

## Audience and experience

Employees are the primary audience. The September headcount baseline was 240; attendance in the current event plan takes precedence. The event may become much smaller as teams confirm their own celebrations. Room layout, service format and staffing should scale together rather than retaining a large-event setup for a small dinner.

People will arrive from different offices and may not know the venue. The first five minutes should be straightforward: one entrance, a visible host, a place for coats and an obvious route to the room. Guests should not need to search an email thread to understand where to go. Any accessibility route should be as clearly described as the main entrance.

The invitation should feel warm and considered. Keep the venue, date, time and RSVP information easy to read. Do not include internal planning notes, supplier prices or unresolved alternatives in the guest-facing copy.

## Event shape

The planning date is Friday, December 11, with guest arrival at 18:00 Eastern. End time, room access and supplier load-out remain working assumptions until the venue confirms them. The desired program is arrival and conversation, dinner, a short set of team awards, a brief presentation and an unhurried close.

For a larger seated dinner, allocate enough time for every table to be served before starting a speech. For a small gathering, a single host can manage a simpler sequence. Avoid announcing a formal stage program unless the room, microphone and staffing support it.

The current notes and approved operating cards carry the final choices. This PRD describes the intended experience rather than freezing the original attendance or supplier lineup.

## Requirements

### Arrival

Use one guest-facing address and a named entrance. Check the walk from transit or drop-off to the room. The organizer needs to know whether there is a coat area, step-free access, a lift, a loading entrance and a guest entrance that differs from the building's postal address.

A delayed guest should be able to join quietly. Keep the check-in point out of the main service path. Name badges are optional; a full registration desk is unnecessary for a small internal group.

### Food

Food must accommodate the dietary requirements in the current event plan. Halal, vegetarian and kosher are separate requirements and should never be treated as interchangeable. Where a supplier cannot meet a requirement, preserve the guest's participation through a separately sourced meal or a reviewed alternative.

Dietary meals should have an owner and a delivery method. A vague note that alternatives are available is insufficient. Confirm labeling, separate utensils where needed, who receives the meals and whether a sealed meal can remain sealed until handed to the guest. Do not put individual dietary information into a public invitation or staff-wide message.

### Program

The awards section should be audible without becoming theatrical. Keep it short enough that food stays warm and service remains calm. The presenter needs a microphone suited to the room, a visible cue from the event lead and a reliable way to advance slides.

The production plan should include a short no-screen fallback. If projection fails, awards can continue from printed notes. The event should not stop while someone finds an adapter.

### Staff

Every operational responsibility needs a role rather than an invented person's name. The organizing team will assign names later. A small event can combine roles, but one person should not be expected to run check-in, receive catering and operate the presentation at the same moment.

### Atmosphere

Warm lighting, low music during conversation and clear sightlines are preferred. Reduce or pause background music for speeches. Avoid decorative items that obstruct a table, interfere with serving or introduce an installation requirement the venue has not accepted.

## Decisions and approval

The event lead can revise working documents and propose arrangements. A supplier change, added cost, cancellation or guest-facing update needs an explicit reviewed action. A quote is not a booking. A cancellation request does not release a deposit or an outstanding commitment until the supplier confirms its terms.

Finance needs a usable estimate with pending amounts visible. A blank quote should remain unknown. Do not make an event look cheaper by treating unpriced work as zero or by subtracting a deposit twice.

Routine edits should be consolidated. If a venue change and a guest-count change happen close together, suppliers should receive one accurate request after the organizer has reviewed the resulting plan.

## Success measures

- Guests can identify when and where to arrive from one invitation.
- The room and service format fit the current attendance.
- Each dietary requirement has a practical meal plan before final guest copy is sent.
- The staff brief names the handoff for arrival, catering, awards and close.
- There is one current budget and one current run of show.
- The event lead spends the evening hosting rather than relaying preventable messages.

## Open dependencies

Venue access, supplier availability, the exact meal offering and the final presentation length require current answers. Room capacity must match the selected room and layout; a building's maximum standing capacity does not establish seated dinner capacity. A published equipment list can inform a plan but does not establish availability on our event date.
