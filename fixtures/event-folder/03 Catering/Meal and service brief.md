# Meal and service brief

Owner: People Operations · Working requirements

## Guest needs

Use the current event count and dietary notes. Vegetarian, halal and kosher requests must be checked separately with the supplier. Do not promise certification or allergen safety based on a restaurant's name, a menu category or general cuisine.

For a separately sourced kosher meal, clarify whether it arrives sealed, how it should be stored and whether the venue can handle it as required. Assign a receiving role. Keep guest-specific information in the restricted organizer notes rather than the general invitation.

## Service shape

For a seated dinner, the caterer should explain whether service is plated, buffet or family style, how long it will take and how many servers are included. The event staffing estimate should not double-count supplier staff, but a supplier's delivery driver should not be assumed to provide service.

Ask whether the quote covers serving utensils, plates, cutlery, napkins, water, cleanup, delivery, tax and gratuity. A per-person headline price alone is insufficient for the budget.

## Supplier change

Before replacing a supplier, keep the original commitment visible and request the cancellation terms. Compare the new supplier's total including delivery, retained deposits and dietary supplements. Do not remove the old cost simply because an inquiry went to a replacement.

If a new menu changes the service style, revise the floor plan, meal timing and staff handoff. The invitation should only name a confirmed supplier or menu. Staff need the practical meal-service change, not the full negotiation history.

## Quiet program transitions

Avoid clearing plates, moving metal service equipment or making noisy deliveries during awards. Reserve a natural pause before the presentation so the room can settle. When service is late, protect the guest experience by moving informal conversation ahead of the formal segment.
