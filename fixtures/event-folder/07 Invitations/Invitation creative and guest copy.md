# Invitation creative and guest copy

Owner: Internal Communications · Working brief

## Direction

An intimate winter evening: candlelight, deep plum, warm cream, a small festive table and a sense of gathering. The artwork should feel like a thoughtfully printed invitation, with restrained decoration and a clear hierarchy. Avoid a large company-logo treatment or promotional language.

The generated Northstar holiday image is the visual anchor. Keep dates, location and meal details in editable invitation fields so a venue change does not require regenerating the illustration. Verify any words baked into the artwork before using it for another event.

## Guest-facing information

Show the event title, date, arrival time, venue, useful arrival directions and RSVP details. If the meal is still being arranged, keep the copy general rather than promising a supplier or dietary accommodation that has not been confirmed.

Suggested tone: “Join us for dinner, a few thank-yous, and an evening with the team.” Keep updates just as direct: state what changed and what the guest needs to do, if anything.

## Update discipline

Consolidate closely related edits into one invitation update. Internal capacity checks, budget estimates, staff assignments and vendor negotiations do not belong in guest notifications. A guest does not need an email because an internal equipment line changed.

The artwork, the working invitation copy and the delivered invitation are separate artifacts. Saving an image in this folder does not establish that Evite or Partiful has received it. Delivery should be recorded with the destination and the version actually used.
